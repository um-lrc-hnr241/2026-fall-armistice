Background

A brief orientation to the thinkers and series discussed in this portfolio.

G. W. F. Hegel

Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel (1770–1831) was a German philosopher. His Phenomenology of Spirit (1807) follows the development of consciousness as it comes to understand itself and its relation to the world.

One influential episode, often called the master–slave dialectic, examines how self-consciousness depends on recognition by another. The work’s account of experience and changing forms of understanding offers one lens for reading characters who struggle to understand themselves and others.

Frantz Fanon

Frantz Fanon (1925–1961) was a Martinican psychiatrist, writer, and anti-colonial thinker. His work examines the psychological and social effects of colonialism, racism, and dehumanization.

In Black Skin, White Masks (1952), Fanon explores how racialized social relations shape lived experience and identity. His writing can help readers ask how systems of power define whose perspective and humanity are recognized. Fanon’s historical and political context is distinct; comparisons should attend to those differences rather than treat unlike experiences as interchangeable.

Westworld

Westworld is a science-fiction television series created by Jonathan Nolan and Lisa Joy, inspired by Michael Crichton’s 1973 film. Its first two seasons are set largely in a Western-themed amusement park populated by artificial beings called hosts and visited by human guests.

Across these seasons, the series raises questions about consciousness, memory, agency, exploitation, and who is treated as a person. Those questions provide the fictional context for the essays in this portfolio.
